# Positioning and inference audit

Read-only analysis of every Markdown file in `content/pages/` and `posts/`, completed 2026-09-30. The implementation decisions are in [PLAN-POSITIONING-AND-EDITORIAL-ARCHITECTURE.md](PLAN-POSITIONING-AND-EDITORIAL-ARCHITECTURE.md). Home-page suggestions from the original review are excluded from that plan.

## Executive assessment

The corpus credibly establishes software engineering and technical leadership. It also shows a sustained interest in how interfaces communicate with people. What it does less clearly is explain why these belong to one professional practice. A reader must assemble that relationship from About and scattered posts. The strongest opportunity outside the home page is to make a few connections visible while tightening the factual and technical accuracy of posts that serve as professional evidence.

This is an expert inference audit, not visitor research. No traffic, keyword, or outcome data was available. Search-language observations are editorial SEO hypotheses. The repository does not document measurable adoption, retention, revenue, or conversion effects.

The page sequence supports a credible career arc: contract software work from circa 2010, increasing leadership, several teams on firm-wide initiatives at McKinsey, and independent work that still carries decisions through implementation. The repository supports consumer-goods and real-estate industry examples. It does **not** provide publishable detail for the intended wider claims about audience scale, adopted properties, campaign work, informational experiences, or internal tooling. Those remain evidence gaps.

The posts cluster into engineering fundamentals, engineering tradeoffs, frontend behavior and delivery, technical leadership, interface communication, and open education. The first four clusters are better evidenced than interface communication. The corpus contains no post that would justify presenting AI interaction as an established editorial specialization.

## Title and excerpt assessment

The titles and excerpts for SOLID, gzipping, DOM, technical debt, feature toggles, and Infinity Labs have adequate information scent and generally match their articles. The resilient-team excerpt clearly explains the coordination thesis, even though its title overstates it. The dependencies excerpt makes a stronger one-combination claim than the body supports. The biomimetics excerpt treats five proposed traits as if they were established success criteria. The tech-leading title and excerpt foreground voice over its practical lessons. Welcome’s title and excerpt are too broad to identify an introductory note. Specific proposed replacements and dependencies appear in the plan.

This is **semantic/editorial SEO reasoning**, not verified search demand. Titles and excerpts should give readers a faithful preview; search engines may display generated title links or snippets instead of supplied metadata.

## Tag and link architecture

The 31 existing assignments span 29 terms. The only repeated terms are `Software Architecture` and `Software Engineering`. Similar concepts are separated among `Tech Lead`, `Technical Leadership`, `Engineering Management`, `Resilient Teams`, and `Career Growth`; among `Web Development`, `JavaScript`, and browser-specific subjects; and among `UX Design`, `Interface Design`, and `Biomimetics`. Many narrowly scoped labels are used only once. Tag vocabulary currently signals breadth more than coherent areas of practice.

The plan proposes 13 familiar canonical terms, with a few deliberate one-post exceptions for specific, recognizable subjects. Their purpose is archive clarity, internal discovery, and semantic consistency. Tags are **not** assumed to be direct ranking factors. The existing About link labeled “Both are still open” points to the site root, rather than to material on either question. The resilient-team article links to an external copy of a dependency article hosted locally. These are the clearest link repairs outside the home page.

## Strong material and evidence limits

Preserve the About line about carrying decisions through implementation; Contact’s problem-led opening and visitor editing controls; the feature-toggle implementation walkthrough; the technical-debt metaphor; the leadership advice on sharing recognition, stakeholder context, and ownership; the biomimetics article’s original question; and the education initiative as supporting work.

Use the following limits when reviewing the plan:

- The corpus establishes the career trajectory and named organization, but does not provide a case study with independently described product outcomes.
- Potential consequences such as better comprehension, trust, adoption, or retention can be discussed as mechanisms or hypotheses; the corpus does not quantify them.
- The biomimetics framework is a proposed way to think, not a validated measure of interface quality.
- The contact assistant is an interaction example, not by itself evidence of a broader AI-interface practice.
- Older posts should remain historically recognizable. Editing should correct material accuracy and positioning problems, not make them sound newly written.
