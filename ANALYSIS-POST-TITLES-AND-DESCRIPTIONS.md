# Post metadata editorial review — titles, descriptions, and tags

**Status:** Proposed decision record; no post metadata has been changed. **Scope:** all 11 Markdown files in `posts/`, limited to their frontmatter `title`, `excerpt` (the site's article description), and ordered `tags`. Recommendation IDs are stable within this review; existing IDs retain their original decisions. The [implementation plan](PLAN-POST-TITLES-AND-DESCRIPTIONS.md) records the proposed sequence and checks.

## Strategic assessment

The journal already demonstrates substantial engineering range: programming principles, browser fundamentals, performance, architecture, delivery, technical leadership, and an exploratory HCI-oriented essay. Most titles give a reader a useful topic cue, and most descriptions add a distinct reason to read. Their variety is an asset; making every article sound like an HCI article would misrepresent the work and flatten the voice.

The material gap is narrower. A few metadata pairs state a stronger conclusion than their articles establish, while the journal introduction gives little indication that interface development sits alongside systems work. The current tag vocabulary also sometimes foregrounds a generic frame (“Career Growth,” “Best Practices”) where the article has a more precise engineering subject. The proposed changes clarify what each piece actually offers and let the engineering-to-human-interaction progression emerge across the collection. They do **not** retrofit later positioning into early technical articles or imply that the biomimetics essay is an empirically validated framework.

Titles and excerpts appear in the journal listing and post summaries; `featured: true` posts can also appear in the Abstract hero rail. Tag order is consequential: `helpers/postContent.ts` makes `tags[0]` the summary's `topic`, which is displayed in article cards and the post page when no `primaryTopic` is set (none of these posts sets one). The complete tag array also becomes article metadata and JSON-LD keywords. The site does **not** currently expose a tag-filtering navigation system, so this review makes no claim that secondary tags improve on-site browsing or search ranking. The rationale below applies information-scent, vocabulary, and text-coherence research to these short entry points. It does not claim that the proposed wording has been experimentally tested or will improve clicks, adoption, or trust.

## Final synthesis

**Current perception:** A technically broad journal with credible implementation and leadership pieces, plus one provocative interface essay. Some previews are more categorical than the articles beneath them, and the visible tags sometimes foreground generic career or method labels over their actual subjects.

**Intended perception:** An engineer and technical lead who writes precisely about systems and implementation, while also exploring how engineered interfaces meet human behavior. Topic labels make that range legible without presenting every technical article as HCI work.

**Main positioning gap:** The collection already contains that arc; several titles, descriptions, and first-tag cues need to describe it with the same precision as the best technical posts.

**Highest-leverage recommendations:** DEPENDENCY-01, LEADING-01, RESILIENT-01, BIOMIMETIC-01, BIOMIMETIC-02, BIOMIMETIC-03, WELCOME-01.
